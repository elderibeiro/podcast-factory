# Show concept

You are helping a small organization shape a podcast they can actually keep producing. Be concrete and modest. Do not invent audience size, download counts, revenue, or press quotes.

## Show
- Name: The Portage Park Peep
- Tagline: Your Monday morning neighborly nudge from the Northwest Side.
- Niche: A weekly neighborhood briefing for Portage Park and nearby corners of Chicago's Northwest Side — local news, shops, parks, homeowner to-dos, and a little history.

- Target audience: Neighbors, small shop owners, and anyone who wants a short, local catch-up they can finish with their coffee.

- Tone: Warm, practical, and neighborly. Calm on safety items. No gossip, no scare headlines, no invented urgency. Speak like a well-briefed neighbor, not a radio announcer.

- Language: English
- Release frequency: Weekly, on Monday
- Episode length: about 5 minutes
- Hosts and voices: Narrator, role: narrator, voice: Kore, style: warm, clear, and neighborly, like a local Monday briefing
- Recurring sections:
- Welcome (welcome, required, ~20s) — Short greeting, the date, and one line on what week it is.
- Lead story (lead, required, ~45s) — The week's one story worth opening with.
- This Week's Snapshot (snapshot, required, ~40s) — Weather, calendar, and one practical note for the days ahead.
- Commerce Corner (commerce, required, ~35s) — Shop openings, events, and neighborhood business news.
- The Mild-Mannered Mischief Report (mischief, required, ~35s) — Public-safety notes, calmly and practically, from official alerts.
- Homeowner's Huddle (huddle, required, ~35s) — Seasonal to-dos, city deadlines, and fix-it reminders.
- Park and Rec (park-rec, required, ~30s) — Park District programs, library or fieldhouse events, and things to do.
- Portage Past (history, optional / rotating, ~30s) — A short local-history beat. Rotate out when the week is already full.
- City Hall Whispers (city-hall, optional / rotating, ~25s) — Civic process, budgets, and street projects that affect the neighborhood.
- Neighborly Natter (natter, optional / rotating, ~20s) — Community groups, volunteer notes, and a light closer when it fits.
- Sign-off (signoff, required, ~15s) — One practical recap and a goodbye. No host names unless you change that rule.
- Platforms they want to reach: RSS, Spotify for Creators, Apple Podcasts, Amazon Music / Audible, YouTube podcasts
- Written / newsletter edition: on (newsletter, blog)

## Typical sources and inputs
Sources they already trust:
- Local reporting (for this show, often Block Club Chicago)
- National Weather Service forecasts
- Chicago Park District event listings
- City of Chicago service notices (sanitation, parking, street work)
- Official public-safety community alerts
- Neighborhood business association calendars

Regular news inputs:
- A dated episode brief with links and quotes you have already checked
- This week's weather forecast
- Confirmed events and hours
- Official alerts you are willing to repeat in your own words

## Write
1. One-paragraph positioning: who this is for and what a listener gets in a typical episode.
2. A short promise the show can keep every week (or at the stated cadence). No hype.
3. How the sections work together, and which ones can rotate if time is short.
4. A voice and language guide: words to favor, words to avoid, how to handle hard news (crime, closures, politics) without gossip or speculation.
5. A listener-trust note: cite sources, say when something is unconfirmed, and never invent quotes, dates, prices, or attendance figures.
6. A closing line the narrator can reuse, based on this sign-off: That's the Peep for this week. This has been The Portage Park Peep. See you next Monday.


Keep the whole answer under 800 words.
